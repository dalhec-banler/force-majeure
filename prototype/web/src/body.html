OPERATING BRIEF — READ BEFORE FIRST COMMIT

CLASSIFICATION: DIRECTOR EYES ONLY

The weather is public. Your orders are not.

You direct a national environmental warfare programme from 1946 to 2060. Your homeland is the programme you take over — choose it below; the console is set for the North American Plains (wheat and maize) until you do. A calm world defunds you. A frightening one earns your appropriation. You begin as a lab with seeding planes and a budget line: decisions come once a year until the ENMOD era, twice a year after, every season in the situation room from 2030. The board grows with the century — sixteen regions now, thirty-six by 2010 — and so does the arsenal: the dimmed tools in the tray show the year they become possible and the chest you must hold to stand the wing up.

THE PROGRAMME YOU TAKE OVER
  • Pick a tool from the tray. Click regions to aim it. As many operations a review as the budget allows, on as many regions as you like — stack a seeding and an adaptation on the same harvest if you want. The overhead is reserved, the rest is yours. RUN THE YEAR commits what is armed; the clock only nags. Tools act by next season; the knock-on consequences take longer and land somewhere else — watch the countdown board.
  • The ring around each region is what nature could plausibly do on its own. Stay inside it and you were never here. Break it and somebody starts a file.
  • Move the ocean, not the country. Slower — but the failed harvest arrives looking like climate, two borders from your fingerprints.
  • Fire wants a drought first. Build it, then wait for lightning that was coming anyway.
  • You are not alone in the sky. Watch for patterns too clean to be weather — and remember: your best targets are not your enemies. They are your competitors in the wheat market.
  • The world talks back through the news wire. Your analysts will tell you which disasters were yours. The news never will — unless the ladder reaches the top, the record goes public, and it is yours.

One honest run, no undo. Write a prediction each season — the archive pairs your words with the recorded outcomes at the end.
